What a bar needs

Everything faces the back-bar.

Four things we settle before anyone chooses a finish.

  1. 01

    The back-bar is the stage

    The bottles are lit as the set and the room is kept darker, so every eye goes to where the drinks are made.

  2. 02

    A speed rail at every station

    Drawn with your head bartender: well, rail, ice, glass wash and garnish, all within reach. Two bartenders can hold 1am.

  3. 03

    Lighting on scenes

    Early, service and late, on a timer. The room changes through the night and nobody reaches for a switch mid-shift.

  4. 04

    Finishes for citrus, sugar and 2am

    Bar tops sealed against acid, brass that takes a polish and upholstery that wipes down at close.

Questions

What owners ask us first.

More questions
Do you handle licences and permits?

We draw what your landlord and the authorities need — fire and exit layouts, extraction routes, signage permissions, mall fit-out submissions — and design to those conditions from day one, so nothing is ripped out later. The licences themselves (in India: food, trade, excise, fire NOC) are held in your name. We work alongside your licensing consultant, or introduce one we trust.

Will I know what it looks like before it’s built?

Yes. You walk through it in 3D, handle every finish on a sample board, and for the pieces that matter most — the counter, a banquette, the bar top — we build a full-size section before we make the rest.

How long does it take?

A 100–150 m² café usually takes 10–14 weeks from signed design to opening: 4–6 weeks of drawings and approvals, then 6–8 weeks on site. Restaurants and bars with full kitchens and extraction take 16–24 weeks. The build is rarely the critical path. Approvals and long-lead items are: kitchen equipment, custom lighting, stone. We order those early.
