02 — Bars

Rooms built for late.

Cocktail bars, wine bars, rooftops, hotel lobbies that turn into something else after ten. We design for the room at its loudest — and make sure it still looks good in the morning.

7pm
Act one

Bright, open, easy

Early doors: a couple at the bar, a table of four in the window. Light levels high enough to read a menu, seating that doesn't feel empty when it's half full.

10pm
Act two

The room tightens

Scenes drop, the back-bar becomes the brightest thing in the room, standing space opens up around the bar without blocking service. This is the moment the layout earns its keep.

1am
Act three

Loud, close, glowing

Finishes that shrug off a spilled Negroni, acoustics that let the music lift without shouting, a bar front that still photographs at ISO 3200. Designed for the night you actually have.

What we obsess over

The back-bar is the stage.

Everything in a bar faces one way. We design the back-bar as the set: lit, layered, built for speed — and the rest of the room as the audience.

  • — Bar ergonomics worked out with your head bartender: reach, speed rail, ice, glass
  • — Standing-to-seated ratios tuned to your licence and your peak
  • — Lighting scenes on a schedule, so the room changes without anyone touching a switch
  • — Acoustic absorption hidden in the places nobody photographs
  • — Finishes chosen for citrus, sugar and 2am
