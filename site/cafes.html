What a café needs

Built for the queue at 08:15.

Four things we settle before anyone chooses a finish.

  1. 01

    The counter comes first

    Order, make, pick-up, bin, in that order along one run. Two baristas do the work of three, and the queue never crosses the pick-up.

  2. 02

    Daylight on the pastry case

    The case goes where the morning light lands, and warm lamps take over after lunch. Pastry sells on how it looks.

  3. 03

    Power where people sit

    Sockets at the bench and along the window ledge, where laptops actually land. Nobody runs a cable across the aisle.

  4. 04

    A corner table

    Every good café has one seat someone claims every morning. We draw it early, and we light it last.

  1. 01 The drawing

    Week 1 · Measured survey

    Counter on the brick wall, so the queue forms along it and not across the door. The stair stays where it is.

  2. 02 The sketch

    Week 2 · Concept

    Drawn from the door: the trusses, the steel stair, a ceiling of plants to walk in under.

  3. 03 The shell

    Week 4 · Strip-out and making good

    Brick cleaned back, trusses painted, the mezzanine made safe. Everything stays white until it’s decided.

  4. 04 Joinery & fit-out

    Weeks 6–9 · Joinery and FF&E

    The steel stair goes in first, then the counter and the bench. Planters next, and the tables work round them.

  5. 05 Finishes

    Weeks 10–11 · Floor, brick, paint

    Screed polished, brick sealed, walls in a warm white that holds the morning light. Beech tops, a pine counter.

  6. 06 Lights on

    Week 12 · Lighting

    Bare lamps on long flex at 2,700 K. 300 lux on the counter, softer everywhere else.

  7. 07 Open

    Day one · 08:00

    Doors at eight. The queue moves.

Questions

What owners ask us first.

More questions
How long does it take?

A 100–150 m² café usually takes 10–14 weeks from signed design to opening: 4–6 weeks of drawings and approvals, then 6–8 weeks on site. Restaurants and bars with full kitchens and extraction take 16–24 weeks. The build is rarely the critical path. Approvals and long-lead items are: kitchen equipment, custom lighting, stone. We order those early.

Can you work while we’re open?

Yes. Most refreshes happen that way. We phase the works around trading: nights, your quiet day, or a short planned closure for the noisy parts. Joinery is made off-site, so time on site is days rather than weeks. We agree dust, noise and access rules with you before anyone picks up a drill.

Is my project too small?

Probably not. A counter, a lighting fix or one wall of joinery can change how a room trades. They’re on the menu as small plates, and they get the same senior team as a flagship.
